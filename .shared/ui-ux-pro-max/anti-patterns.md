# Anti-patterns — the AI-default looks to avoid

Right now machine-generated design clusters around a few recognizable looks. They are
legitimate for *some* briefs, but they show up regardless of subject — which is what makes
them defaults rather than choices.

## The three usual suspects

1. **Warm cream + serif + terracotta** — near-`#F4F1EA` background, high-contrast serif
   display, single terracotta/clay accent.
2. **Near-black + one acid accent** — `#0A0A0A`-ish canvas with a lone bright acid-green or
   vermilion highlight.
3. **Broadsheet** — hairline rules, zero border-radius, dense newspaper-style columns,
   all-caps eyebrows.

If you find yourself reaching for one of these *without the brief asking for it*, stop and
choose something grounded in the subject instead.

## Other tells

- "Big number + tiny label + gradient accent" hero when the content isn't actually about
  that metric.
- Numbered markers (01 / 02 / 03) on content that isn't an ordered sequence.
- Three-up card grids as the reflex layout for any collection.
- Generic gradient blobs / mesh backgrounds with no tie to the subject.
- Glassmorphism + blur applied everywhere instead of one deliberate surface.
- Scattered hover/scroll animations with no orchestration (more ≠ better).
- Filler copy that sells ("Empower your workflow") instead of describing.
- System-centric labels ("Submit", "Config", "Entity") instead of user-centric verbs.

## The test

Before committing a decision, ask: *"If I gave this same brief to a generic generator,
would it land here too?"* If yes, and the brief didn't demand it, revise — and record what
you changed and why. The brief's explicit instructions always win; only spend the *free*
axes on something distinctive.
